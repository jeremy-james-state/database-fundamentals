A key is how you tell rows apart and how you point at them later.

Start with a super key: any set of columns that is unique for every row.

The whole row is a super key if no two rows are identical.

That is a weak compliment.

You want a smaller set.

A candidate key is a super key with no leftover column you could drop and still be unique.

email might be a candidate if you truly never repeat it.

customer_id might be another candidate.

You pick one candidate to be the primary key.

The others can stay UNIQUE.

A good primary key is unique, always present, and stable.

Stable means it does not change because marketing renamed a product code.

Natural keys come from the world: ISBN, email, government id.

They are meaningful, and they change when the world changes.

Surrogate keys are invented: an integer the database assigns.

They are dull, and that is the point.

They do not encode the customer's city.

They do not change when the email changes.

Prefer a surrogate for the primary key when the natural ones wiggle.

Still put UNIQUE on the natural candidates you care about.

ISBN should stay unique even if book_id is the primary key.
