An entity-relationship diagram is a map of tables before they become SQL, or beside the SQL.

A box is an entity.

The name on the box is the table you expect.

Inside the box you list attributes.

You mark the primary key.

You mark foreign keys as copies of someone else's primary key.

A line is a relationship.

The ends of the line carry cardinality: one or many.

A crow's foot means many.

A single bar means one.

A circle means zero is allowed.

A second bar means one is required.

You do not need every symbol on day one.

You need boxes, keys, and honest lines.

Read a line as a sentence.

If you cannot say the sentence, the line is decoration.

Do not draw a line labeled after a one-off demo.

Draw the store you are actually building.

Keep example names ordinary: customers, orders, books.

A diagram that uses joke names will be quoted as if they were tables.

Attributes that are only display labels stay off the line.

An entity-relationship diagram is a picture of what you will store and how those things connect, drawn before CREATE TABLE hardens a guess.

Entities are the things: customer, product, order.

They appear as rectangles.

Attributes are the facts inside the rectangle: name, email, price, with types once you know them.

Relationships are how entities participate: a customer places an order, an order contains products.

Chen notation uses diamonds for relationships, and it showed up in a 1976 paper, which is why textbooks still teach it.

Crow's foot notation uses lines and little marks at the ends for cardinality, and practitioners sketch it on whiteboards because it is fast.

Neither is more true.

Switching mid-diagram is what is false.

A complete diagram names the entities, the attributes you will actually store, the relationships, and the cardinalities once you know them.

It is a map, not a screenshot of the application.

Screens have buttons.

Diagrams have identity and connection.

Start by placing the entities you already found.

Then ask, for each pair that your interviews connected, whether a relationship belongs.

Not every pair gets a line.

Customers and manufacturers might only meet through products.

Draw the real verb.

places, contains, reviews, works for.

If you cannot name the verb, you do not have a relationship yet.

You have two boxes on a poster.

Cardinality marks wait for the next lessons.

Here the skill is connecting without spaghetti: every line is a sentence from requirements.

The benefit is forgetting less.

A missing line is a join you will fake with a comment in six months.

An extra line is a constraint you cannot explain.

Read the diagram out loud as sentences.

If a sentence was never said in gathering, erase the line.

If a sentence was said and there is no line, draw it.

Then you are ready to talk about one, many, and identifying relationships.
